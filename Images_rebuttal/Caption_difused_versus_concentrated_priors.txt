**Difused BART prior vs. Concentrated BART prior**: Comparison of prediction regions constructed by EPISCORE using BART with a diffused prior and BART with a concentrated prior. The experiment considers simulated data composed of high- and low-density regions (80% of the samples uniformly distributed in \([-1, 0]\) and 20% following a Beta(5, 5) distribution in \([0, 1]\)), with a conditional mean given by a step function (\( \mathbb{1}_{x > 0} \)) and heteroscedastic variance defined by \( \sigma(x) = 0.05 + \sin^2(15x)\mathbb{1}_{x > 0} \). The data is split into training, calibration, and test sets, each containing 200 samples. The base model is a 10-nearest neighbors (K-NN) regression trained on the training dataset. For EPISCORE, both BART priors share the same parameters: \( m = 50 \), heteroscedastic variance, normal error distribution, normalized response, and 1000 Monte Carlo samples. In the Diffused BART prior, a parameter \( \alpha = 0.9 \) is used. In contrast, EPISCORE with the Concentrated BART prior uses \( \alpha = 0.1 \). In summary, the concentration of the prior is controlled by the alpha value. In the figure, we observe that the prediction region decreases significantly with a highly concentrated prior. In conclusion, the inclusion of a concentrated prior has a great impact on the prediction region, making it more accurate.
