**Diffuse BART Prior vs. Concentrated BART Prior:**  
This figure compares prediction regions produced by EPICSCORE using BART under two prior configurations: a diffuse prior and a concentrated prior. The experiment is based on simulated data that includes both high- and low-density regions: 80% of the samples are uniformly drawn from [-1, 0], while the remaining 20% follow a Beta(5, 5) distribution in [0, 1]. The conditional mean is defined by a step function (𝟙_{x > 0}), and the variance is heteroscedastic, given by σ(x) = 0.05 + sin²(15x)𝟙_{x > 0}.

The dataset is split into training, calibration, and test sets, each with 200 samples. The base predictor is a k-nearest neighbors (k-NN) regression model with k = 10, trained on the training set. For EPICSCORE, both BART priors use the same settings: m = 50 trees, heteroscedastic variance, normal likelihood, response normalization, and 1000 Monte Carlo samples. The key difference lies in the α hyperparameter, which controls tree depth: α = 0.9 for the diffuse prior and α = 0.1 for the concentrated prior.

The figure illustrates that the diffuse prior produces wider prediction regions—particularly in sparse areas—due to greater model flexibility, whereas the concentrated prior yields narrower intervals reflecting stronger regularization. This highlights the sensitivity of EPICSCORE to prior specification and demonstrates how prior concentration directly influences the adaptiveness and sharpness of the prediction sets.
