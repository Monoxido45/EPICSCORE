**HPD split vs. EPICSCORE:**  
Comparison of prediction regions generated by the HPD (Highest Predictive Density) split and EPICSCORE methods in a simulated scenario designed to reflect both high epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. The data consists of two distinct regions: 80% of the samples are drawn uniformly from \([-1, 0]\), representing a dense region, while the remaining 20% follow a \(\text{Beta}(5, 5)\) distribution on \([0, 1]\), capturing a sparser, high-uncertainty area. The conditional mean is defined by a step function (\(\mathbb{1}_{x > 0}\)), with heteroscedastic noise specified as \(\sigma(x) = 0.05 + \sin^2(15x)\mathbb{1}_{x > 0}\).

The base conditional density model is a Mixture Density Network (MDN) with architecture 1–64–128–5 (input–hidden layers–mixture components), trained on 2000 samples. EPICSCORE uses a BART model with 50 trees, gamma error distribution, and heteroscedastic variance, calibrated using 1000 posterior samples. The HPD region is derived directly from the MDN posterior.

Both methods produce similar regions in the high-density area (\(x < 0\)), but EPICSCORE exhibits sharper adaptivity in the sparse region (\(x > 0\)), offering more informative and expanded predictive sets where epistemic uncertainty is higher. This highlights EPICSCORE’s improved capability for uncertainty quantification in challenging, data-scarce settings.
