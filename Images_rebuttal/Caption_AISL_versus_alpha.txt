AISL vs. α: *Average Interval Score Loss* (AISL) as a function of the α hyperparameter in the BART model used within the EPICSCORE framework. The experiment uses simulated data containing both high- and low-density regions: 80% of the samples are uniformly distributed in [-1, 0], and 20% follow a Beta(5, 5) distribution in [0, 1]. The conditional mean is defined by a step function (𝟙_{x > 0}), and the heteroscedastic variance is given by σ(x) = 0.05 + sin²(15x)𝟙_{x > 0}. A *k*-NN model with 10 neighbors serves as the base predictor, trained on 200 samples. The calibration and test sets consist of 200 and 500 samples, respectively. For each value of α, the EPICSCORE model is calibrated and evaluated over 10 repetitions, using 1000 Monte Carlo samples per repetition. The plot demonstrates how varying α — which controls prior tree depth in BART — impacts the quality of predictive intervals, with lower AISL values indicating better performance in terms of the tradeoff.
