**AISL vs. α:**  
This figure presents the variation of the *Average Interval Score Loss* (AISL) as a function of the α hyperparameter in the BART model used within the EPICSCORE framework. The underlying experiment is based on simulated data with distinct density regions: 80% of the samples are drawn uniformly from [-1, 0] (dense region), while the remaining 20% are sampled from a Beta(5, 5) distribution on [0, 1] (sparse region). The conditional mean follows a step function (𝟙_{x > 0}), and the noise is heteroscedastic, defined as σ(x) = 0.05 + sin²(15x)𝟙_{x > 0}.

A k-NN model with 10 neighbors is used as the base predictor and is trained on 200 samples. The calibration and test sets include 200 and 500 samples, respectively. For each value of α, which governs the expected tree depth in the BART prior, the EPICSCORE model is calibrated and evaluated over 10 independent runs, with 1000 Monte Carlo samples per run.

The figure illustrates how this prior parameter influences the sharpness and coverage tradeoff of the resulting prediction intervals. Lower AISL values indicate more favorable balance, suggesting that tuning α can significantly affect performance by controlling model flexibility and prior regularization.
